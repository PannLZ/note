1 . Introduction
The stock market serves as a relevant indicator of economic performance, representing national economies with its dynamics and
returns, making stock forecasting one of the most challenging tasks [1]. Traditional forecasting approaches have relied on the analysis
of technical indicators, macroeconomic factors, and company-specific data [2]. With the growing availability of heterogeneous data
sources, the focus has shifted toward integrating numerical time series with unstructured textual information [3]. However, effectively

combining these modalities to capture temporal and contextual dependencies in a unified predictive framework remains a significant
challenge, particularly in achieving robust and adaptive stock market forecasting models [4].
In recent years, there has been increasing academic recognition of the critical role investor sentiment plays in shaping financial
markets [5], although it is significantly influenced by several contextual factors (e.g., news events, brand strength, and loyalty).
Sentiment analysis further contributes to sustained investor interest and can impact stock performance [6].
While the traditional text mining methodologies have shown some effectiveness in analyzing content from online stock forums,
they typically depend on labor-intensive preprocessing and the availability of large, labeled datasets [7]. These requirements limit
their scalability and hinder their capacity to reflect the real-time and complex dynamics of investor sentiment. In turn, large language
models (LLMs) such as ChatGPT1 and LLaMA,2 which leverage in-context learning, offer a more flexible and efficient alternative.
These models can extract sentiment with greater nuance from large volumes of financial textual content, eliminating the need for
task-specific supervision and enabling more accurate and timely sentiment-driven analysis of stock performance [7].
Financial time series forecasting plays a crucial role in quantitative finance, providing valuable insights that drive the development
of effective and profitable investment strategies [8]. However, stock price prediction remains a challenging task due to the nonlinear
and nonstationary nature of financial time series, which are shaped by a multitude of dynamic factors [1]. Indeed, price fluctuations
are the result of complex dynamics among macroeconomic policies, societal fundamentals, and investor sentiment [9], contributing
to a highly volatile and uncertain market environment.
1.1 . Gaps
Stock prices are often modeled as univariate time series data, which form the foundation of most forecasting models [10]. Although
these data are continuously recorded, they are often noisy and susceptible to anomalies due to the influence of external events [11].
Additionally, traditional models tend to struggle with generalization, particularly when applied to unseen or out-of-distribution data.
This is largely because one-dimensional time series data offer relatively simplistic features, primarily capturing short-term price
fluctuations without sufficient contextual representation [12]. However, recent advances in computing resources and data processing
have enabled deep learning models to overcome some of these limitations by learning richer, hierarchical representations from raw
time series data, thereby enhancing robustness and predictive accuracy [11].
Despite the progress achieved by deep learning, challenges surrounding universality and interpretability remain central concerns
in stock market forecasting. Many existing neural network-based models exhibit limited generalizability, often performing well only
on specific datasets or under narrow market conditions [13]. However, as economic globalization deepens, financial markets are
no longer isolated systems: they are complex, interdependent networks involving diverse countries, sectors, and asset classes. This
evolving landscape underscores the urgent need for models that are both broadly applicable across varying market contexts and
capable of providing interpretable predictions. Addressing these issues is a key direction for future research in stock forecasting,
especially as the demand grows for AI systems that not only perform well but also offer transparent and trustworthy decision-making
processes.
Most existing research in stock market time series forecasting has concentrated on short-term predictions, typically forecasting
prices on single indices (e.g., [14]).
Beyond these architectural and scope limitations, the integration of LLMs into financial forecasting itself remains hindered by four
concrete unresolved challenges that, taken together, motivate the present study.
First, we can take into account the limitations of domain-specific LLMs. Indeed, open-source financial LLMs (e.g., FinBERT, FinGPT,
InvestLM) are typically smaller models, often trained primarily on formal news content, which limits their generalizability across
diverse financial NLP tasks and unstructured textual environments. Larger proprietary models such as BloombergGPT are not publicly
available, preventing independent assessment by the research community. This motivates the evaluation of general-purpose, publicly
available LLMs (Llama, Mistral, Vicuna) as zero-shot sentiment extractors, which is a topic that has been underexplored.
Another thing to be taken into account is the non-trivial problem of identifying the optimal prompt for sentiment extraction in a
domain characterized by uncertainty, volatility, and non-linear behavior. Different LLMs may respond differently to the same prompt,
and the literature lacks a systematic comparison under identical prompting conditions, a gap our study directly addresses by using a
fixed prompt and varying the LLM.
The third element to be taken into account is the scarcity of high-quality financial-textual datasets, whose building demands rare
combined expertise in financial knowledge and textual semantics, limiting the size and diversity of evaluation corpora reported in the
literature. Prior LLM-based sentiment-augmented forecasting works typically rely on small textual samples (≈ [1, 000]1000 headlines),
which are insufficient for robust comparative conclusions across architectures. Our study uses [8, 652]8652 headlines, providing a
substantially larger evaluation base.
Lastly, the lack of identification of effective predictive features also needs to be considered. Indeed, while many works generate
large numbers of features from historical financial data, these features alone do not always yield accurate forecasts [15]. Whether
and how LLM-derived sentiment features meaningfully complement classical economic indicators across different deep learning
architectures (LSTM, GAN, Transformer) is an open empirical question that no existing study addresses systematically.

The intersection of these four challenges, and their particular relevance to the underexplored domain of meme stocks [16], whose
dynamics are dominated by social-media-driven retail behavior rather than fundamentals, provides the concrete justification for our
research question. Specifically, prior works such as [7] evaluate only a single LLM on approximately [1, 000]1000 news samples,

To address the identified gaps, we combined daily historical prices of 47 companies with sentiment information extracted by LLMs
from news headlines and additional economic indicators to predict daily closing prices using deep learning models.
1.2 . Research contributions
In this paper, we address the challenge of improving stock price forecasting by integrating sentiment analysis of financial news
headlines using LLMs along with complex economic indicators. Our objective is to infer nuanced sentiment signals from unstructured
textual data through LLMs, which are then incorporated into deep learning-based predictive models to enhance forecasting effec­
tiveness. Our approach has been focused on meme stocks that are mostly affected by user behavior on social media. The proposed
framework has practical implications that can be applied across application domains that combine textual data and time series, such
as event detection, e-health diagnosis, and so on.
The main contributions of this work are the following:
• We propose a framework integrating zero-shot LLM-driven sentiment analysis of financial news headlines with historical stock
price data, enhancing the accuracy of closing price prediction;
• We integrate derived economic indicators that capture short-term price momentum, trend direction, and multi-scale temporal
signals obtained through frequency-based analysis;
• We evaluate the performance in terms of effectiveness and efficiency of three LLMs for polarity extraction and three deep learning
architectures within this framework to assess their forecasting capabilities on 47 prominent publicly traded companies across
major global stock exchanges.

learning models. Section 5 details the experimental design, model configurations, and evaluation metrics. Section 6 presents and
discusses the results of our empirical evaluation. Finally, Section 7 concludes the paper and highlights directions for future research.
2 . Related work
Different from general time series forecasting tasks [17], stock price prediction is influenced by the cognitive biases, emotional
responses, and sociopolitical dynamics of chaotic human behavior. Indeed, the rising availability of unstructured textual data, par­
ticularly financial news and social media content, has opened new alternatives for adding sentiment-driven insights into predictive
models [18].
Integrating Natural Language Processing (NLP) with financial forecasting has emerged as a crucial research field, encompassing
several challenges such as the extraction of meaningful sentiment signals from financial text, the temporal alignment and aggregation
of sentiment features with traditional financial indicators, and the development of robust predictive models capable of leveraging
multimodal information sources [19].
In this regard, recent advances in language models have changed the landscape of sentiment analysis, offering opportunities for
understanding financial context without task-specific fine-tuning. However, the application of these models to financial forecasting
implies distinct issues, including handling sparse news coverage, managing temporal relationships in sentiment data, and picking
appropriate architectural frameworks for integrating textual and numerical elements [11].
The approaches to stock market prediction can be characterized depending on their methodological focus and data utilization
methodologies:
1. technical analysis approaches, which concentrate exclusively on past price and volume data, employing mathematical indica­
tors to detect patterns and trends;
2. fundamental analysis techniques, which use company-specific financial measurements, earnings data, and macroeconomic
indicators to assess intrinsic value and predict price movements;
3. quantitative approaches, which apply statistical and machine learning techniques, spanning from conventional econometric
models to sophisticated deep learning-based models, to capture intricate nonlinear connections in financial data;
4. sentiment-driven techniques, which integrate textual input from news items, social media, and financial reports to bring market
psychology and investor behavior into prediction models;
5. hybrid techniques, which integrate several strategies to harness the complementary properties of distinct data sources and
analytical tools.
Recent studies have increasingly focused on relevant textual information to predict stock movement and extract further informative
text features [20]. Indeed, analyzing the emotional orientation of the textual context helps in understanding the stock market’s
emotional context and its dynamic fluctuations more deeply [6]. For instance, Liu et al. [21] propose FinBERT, a pre-trained language
model based on BERT, trained on general and financial corpora using multi-task self-supervised learning across six pre-training tasks,

achieving state-of-the-art performance. Effectively leveraging user-generated content in social communities remains a significant
challenge for sentiment analysis, particularly due to the presence of low-quality comments that may compromise the performance of
approaches that rely on opinion-mining [22].
In recent years, researchers have explored fundamental analysis, sentiment analysis, and related methods, trying to enhance
prediction effectiveness. For instance, the combination of technical indicators and text has been employed to improve predictive
performance and provide deeper insights into market dynamics [11]. Traditional approaches, such as hard fusion, apply simple,
almost parameter-free methods to combine data [23], whereas soft fusion leverages deep learning techniques to extract richer feature
representations and enable deeper interaction between modalities [24]. Another framework has been made by Liu et al. [25], which
proposes a multimodal emotion recognition framework that learns from LLMs via imitation, enabling training on unlabeled real-world
stock market data by generating pseudo labels from textual input.
Whilst the combination of textual and numerical features is receiving much attention, fundamental analysis remains a widely
adopted method in finance since valuable information can be extracted from stock prices. Mining the information cloistered in price
fluctuations still enables the prediction of the stock market’s trend [26].
However, the dynamic and volatile nature of the financial markets poses a major challenge to modeling temporal and non-linear
relationships, which are critical for accurate time series forecasting [27]. Indeed, temporal dynamics embody intricate, evolving
patterns driven by exogenous market events and shifts in investor sentiment. Nonlinearities stem from intricate interdependencies
among financial variables, where even minor fluctuations in one factor may trigger large and unpredictable effects [28]. Stock market
prediction is further hindered by the difficulty of distinguishing signal from noise, data biases (e.g., survivorship, look-ahead bias),
and overfitting risks that complicate model generalization. These factors limit the reliability of forecasts and the practical deployment
of predictive systems in dynamic financial environments.
Another main problem in the finance domain is the feature extraction process, which is often performed manually or semi-
automatically by using appropriate rules [26]. Indeed, existing tools for analyzing investor sentiment are constrained by their reliance
on large, high-quality labeled datasets, the creation of which is both costly and labor-intensive. A further hurdle in designing predictive
models concerns the need for effective representation of temporal patterns in financial time series, which are non-linear, volatile,
and dynamic [4]. Additionally, these models struggle to adapt to the changing dynamics of the market and often fail to capture
the subtle and context-dependent sentiments embedded in unstructured financial text, which limits their overall performance and
practical applicability. This study contributes to the hybrid techniques category by systematically integrating LLM-based sentiment
analysis with traditional technical indicators across multiple deep learning architectures, addressing the fundamental challenge of
multimodal financial forecasting in an era of abundant textual data.
The main novelties of the proposed approach w.r.t. the state of the art are summarized as follows:
• We integrate LLM-driven sentiment analysis with historical financial time-series data and economic indicators to handle the
dynamic behavior of the market, in contrast to prior works such as [15], and [4] which use only historical data and economic
indicators;
• We deal with a financial time series forecasting problem while also considering the sentiment extracted by LLMs. In contrast,
[7] only studies the correlation between LLM-driven sentiment analysis and time series;
• We assess the performance of three different LLM models used for sentiment analysis with a dataset composed of 8652 news
headlines, in contrast to [7] which only uses one LLM and 1000 pre-filtered samples.

4 . Methodology
We design a multimodal framework for financial forecasting, integrating traditional economic indicators with sentiment analysis
scores inferred from LLMs on financial news to predict stock price returns. As shown in Fig. 1, our framework contains three main
steps: data collection, feature engineering, and model training.
In the data ingestion phase, we collect information from two different streams: historical financial information and daily news.
This information is fed as input to the feature engineering phase to infer economic indicators and sentiment polarity of the daily news.
We then aggregate data and perform the training of a deep learning model to predict future stock values.
4.1 . Data ingestion
Both historical price and news headlines data were provided by a private source. The historical price data covered daily open,
high, low, close, and volume records. News headlines, instead, provided publication date, source URL, and company ticker for each
article.
We ingest data from two different data sources, which include historical, financial, and news-related data for 47 meme stocks
listed on major stock exchanges from January 2019 to December 2021. The former is made up of daily records of opening, high, low,
and closing prices and daily exchange volumes, while the latter is composed of 8652 headlines, their publication date, a link to the
source, and the corresponding company acronym. The included enterprises represent a diverse sample of companies (e.g., technology,
transportation), allowing for a realistic and nuanced evaluation of the methodologies proposed in this study.
Table 1 reports the characterization of news for the 47 meme stocks, whose price dynamics are predominantly influenced by social
media-driven narratives rather than traditional fundamental indicators.
The prediction of meme stock prices presents different challenges, primarily due to the dynamic nature of social media-driven
behaviors and the high volatility of these equities, which are strongly influenced by both financial indicators and contextual
factors [16].
The multimodal structure of the dataset enables a comprehensive analysis of possible correlations between sentiment inferred
from news textual content and stock price fluctuations.
4.2 . Feature engineering
The preprocessing pipeline begins with the systematic loading of economic data for each ticker symbol from serialized pickle
files, converting each to a 32-bit floating-point precision. This standardization ensures consistency across securities and improves
memory efficiency during downstream computations. Subsequently, the feature engineering step is performed. This step is divided
into polarity extraction from news headlines and economic indicator extraction from numerical time series. Once both processes are
completed, the results are merged through a data aggregation step, aligning historical financial data with corresponding sentiment
information.

4.2.1 . Polarity extraction
The first step of feature engineering involves the extraction of sentiment elements from each headline. To achieve this, we deploy
three different LLMs (i.e., Llama, Mistral, and Vicuna) in a zero-shot setting, relying exclusively on their general-purpose capabilities
to analyze and assign sentiment labels to financial news headlines.
We prompted the LLMs to categorize headlines into three unique sentiment categories through the prompt shown in Listing 1:
positive, reflecting news that anticipates likely increases in stock values, negative, indicating news that implies probable downward
pressure on stock prices, and neutral, representing news with no major anticipated impact on stock price movements. Furthermore,
LLMs are instructed to report an overall sentiment polarity score in the range of [−1.0, 1.0], establishing a continuous measure of
emotion intensity. These four fields (positive, negative, neutral, and polarity) are schema-enforced in a JSON object. To ensure
logical consistency, we validate the parsed results: if the sum of the three category probabilities falls outside the range [0.99, 1.01],
the response is flagged as a hallucination. In such cases, the model is re-prompted with the same headline until a valid, normalized
distribution is achieved.
To tackle the challenge of the fluctuating influence of news sentiment on stock price movements, we introduce an impact factor
that estimates the potential market effect of daily sentiment. This addition overcomes the limitations of raw sentiment scores by
incorporating news volume and sentiment consistency. Specifically, the impact factor considers the mean polarity of daily headlines,
the volume of news coverage, and the standard deviation of sentiment scores, capturing the intuition that high-volume, polarized
news days are likely to have greater market impact than days with few headlines or consensus. To handle periods without news
coverage, we apply an exponential decay mechanism with a decay rate of 0.05 to propagate the last known sentiment information
forward to empty calendar days, representing the gradual lowering influence of past news over time. Additionally, we assign a neutral
sentiment value (polarity and neutral scores set to 1, negative and positive scores set to 0) to days without news and introduce a
binary flag to distinguish between observed and imputed sentiment, enabling models to differentiate genuine from inferred signals.
Finally, to incorporate company-specific characteristics, we employed a sophisticated embedding approach using the pre-trained
FinBERT [21], which processed each ticker symbol to generate contextual embeddings, allowing us to capture semantic relationships
between different companies while maintaining computational tractability.

4.3 . Stock forecasting
In this section, we first present the proposed data-driven module for stock market forecasting, relying on recent state-of-the-
art approaches according to the classification defined by Bao et al. [18], and, successively, we describe the training procedure in
Section 4.3.1.
LSTM architecture represents a hybrid neural network design that integrates learnable stock embeddings with bidirectional tempo­
ral processing for multi-asset financial forecasting, addressing the dual challenges of capturing company-specific characteristics and
temporal dependencies inherent in financial time series predictions.
GAN architecture employs a dual-network design constituted by a residual generator and discriminator, both containing, as in this
case, learnable stock embeddings. This architecture tries to exploit adversarial training dynamics to capture the intricate patterns and
correlations inherent in multi-asset financial data.
Transformers architecture is built on the attention neural network design that employs self-attention mechanisms and positional
encoding to capture temporal correlations in financial time series data without relying on recurrent components. This model mixes
learnable stock embeddings with multihead self-attention to handle the hard task of simulating long-range interdependence while
preserving company-specific traits. The positional encoding mechanism introduces temporal order information into the embedded
representations, generating, in this way, position-dependent signals through the application of sine functions to even-indexed di­
mensions and cosine functions to odd-indexed ones, creating unique positional signatures for each temporal position within the
sequence.
4.3.1 . Training
The training phase uses a thorough technique designed to provide robust model evaluation and optimal performance across
multiple architectural configurations.
Feature preprocessing comprised a normalization within a range of [1, 6] to ensure numerical stability and to avoid both vanishing
gradient problems and collapsing around zero values. Normalization is performed independently for each ticker symbol to account for
differences in price scales and volatility across stocks. A sliding window approach is used to generate sequential input–output pairs
with a window length of five days. However, to ensure a fair evaluation, the dataset was partitioned into training (70%), validation
(20%), and test (10%) sets.
We train the model following Algorithm 1. The core training process utilized a loss function with L1 regression loss, integrating
an L1 regularization term on linear layer weights to prevent overfitting and maximum error weighting to penalize extreme prediction
errors that could indicate model instability. This multi-component method guaranteed that models addressed the dual nature of finan­
cial forecasting, where both price levels and movement directions hold important practical value. During the training, we employed
the approach of gradient trimming to prevent bursting gradients, a frequent issue in recurrent architectures when processing large
sequences. On the validation dataset, after each training epoch, we evaluated model performance, implementing model checkpointing
based on the decrease in the loss function to preserve only configurations that achieved improved validation performance, as an early
stopping mechanism to prevent overfitting, ensuring optimal model selection.
Multiple performance indicators were simultaneously monitored (i.e., MAE, MSE, RMSE, and $MAPE) alongside percentage errors,
a financial-specific measure, other than computational efficiency metrics related to training and inference time, which provided
insights into the practical deployment viability of different model configurations. AdaGrad was chosen as the optimization algorithm
with an adaptive learning rate schedule based on monitoring the average loss, which automatically reduced learning rates when
validation performance plateaued for consecutive epochs, allowing aggressive initial learning but ensuring convergence stability
in later training phases. We also structured a weight decay regularization offering extra overfitting protection, which is extremely
significant given the complicated feature interactions present in the sentiment-augmented datasets, where high-dimensional feature
spaces could lead to spurious correlations.
At the end of the training phase, we conducted a final evaluation using a completely isolated test dataset that had remained unseen
throughout the training and validation phases. We stored all test metric results as final accuracy model results.

indicators that partially obviate the need for explicit sentiment features while still benefiting from sentiment integration.

These experimental observations align with established theoretical frameworks in financial time-series literature. We further
contextualize our framework’s performance by discussing the results obtained against the theoretical findings of a representative
model taxonomy [18] composed of Recurrent Neural Networks, Transformers, and GANs.
Recurrent models such as LSTMs are widely recognized for their ability to model sequential dependencies and smooth tempo­
ral transitions within noisy financial environments. Their ability to maintain stable regression performance and lower variance in
prediction errors, as evidenced by our lower RMSE dispersion and reduced mean-to-median error ratios [26], is well supported by
recent comparative studies [30]. Similarly, our findings regarding Transformer-based architectures reflect their capacity for capturing
long-range temporal dependencies through self-attention mechanisms [31]. While they proved computationally more demanding in
our tests, their competitive RMSE and superior $MAPE scores suggest an enhanced ability for local trend adaptation and short-term
sensitivity. This confirms that attention-based models can effectively synthesize heterogeneous signals, such as the integration of
sentiment and traditional financial indicators [30]. Conversely, GAN-based forecasting approaches have been previously associated
with higher training instability due to adversarial optimization dynamics [32]. The elevated variance and larger outlier sensitivity
observed in our results are theoretically coherent with the known difficulty of stabilizing GAN training in regression-oriented financial
forecasting scenarios, particularly in environments characterized by heterogeneous volatility regimes [33].

7 . Conclusion and future work
Stock forecasting is a complex and high-impact task, particularly when integrating unstructured data sources such as financial
news. In this work, we have presented a comprehensive framework that leverages LLMs for zero-shot sentiment analysis of news
headlines and integrates these sentiment signals with historical stock price data. We combine this enriched dataset with deep learning
models to improve stock price prediction effectiveness.
The experimental results reveal that large performance disparities exist among LLM sentiment extractors, with Vicuna emerging
as the most successful model for financial sentiment integration despite exhibiting the greatest hallucination rate at 9.6% among the
LLMs utilized by us. This research highlights intrinsic trade-offs between computational efficiency and output reliability that demand
careful consideration for practical deployment scenarios.
By our results, we have seen that architectural selection makes an impact on both prediction accuracy and computational efficiency,
with LSTM models consistently demonstrating superior performance across multiple metrics, achieving the lowest RMSE values while

requiring substantially less computational resources compared to alternative architectures. However, Transformer models achieve
competitive accuracy with superior MAPE performance, even while demanding increased computational overhead, whereas GAN
architectures exhibit fundamental limitations for direct financial forecasting applications despite their theoretical advantages in
capturing complex data distributions.
From this investigation, we found that enhanced dataset integration represents a critical priority, requiring the development of
more comprehensive datasets that incorporate several information sources beyond traditional news headlines, with the integration
of social media sentiment, earnings call transcripts, regulatory filings, and real-time market commentary, to create richer multimodal
datasets while investigating alternative temporal granularities and cross-market sentiment propagation for deeper insights into global
market dynamics.
While our work is focused on meme stocks, recent literature suggests that long-term relationships (e.g., industry linkages) also
have transient effects [4]. This indicates that even for traditional blue-chip stocks, external information (e.g., news, events) can still
play a role, albeit with different temporal dynamics due to challenges in interpreting event semantics [1]. This suggests that the
consistency and contextual interpretation of sentiment remain important factors. Finally, the use of recent NLP methodologies (e.g.,
LLMs) improves the extraction and interpretation of financial textual data, potentially mitigating some of the noise associated with
lower or less reactive news volume [34].
Future improvements in forecasting performance may come from advanced architectures, such as ensembles and lightweight
models augmented with attention mechanisms. These approaches could combine the efficiency of recurrent networks with the long-
range pattern recognition of attention-based models.
From a deployment perspective, real-time implementation and scalability research should be addressed as the main challenges in
developing streaming processing pipelines for real-time sentiment analysis and prediction. This should also take into account different
techniques, such as edge computing solutions and model compression, which can help implement the solution in resource-constrained
environments.
Based on dataset typology, both cross-market and multi-asset extensions could greatly enhance the practical usefulness of
sentiment-augmented forecasting through studying the generalizability of findings across diverse asset classes, geographic market­
places, and economic conditions.
